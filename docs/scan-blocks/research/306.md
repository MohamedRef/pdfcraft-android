# #306 (S20): fingers, glare and stains, from public sources

Labels: **found** (URL and quote), **worked out** (arithmetic shown), **not known** (where looked). Nothing was run. No detector, threshold or fill was tested here. The spec's thresholds (250, 0.2%, 1–12%, 2–6% ring, 6 px feather, chroma 0.05, luminance 8%, YCbCr skin ranges) are design choices in the spec. I found no public source that supports any of them, so each one is **not known** unless stated otherwise.

Sources read:
- A: Google ML Kit document scanner page, https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/doc-scanner.
- B: this repository's handover, `docs/ANDROID_SCAN_HANDOVER.md` (row #307), in `/home/claude/agents/repo`.
- Not read: CamScanner's own pages (the spec cites them; I did not check them), and any inpainting model's licence or size (see Section 5).

## 1. What public sources say about the three defects

- **F1 (found, A).** Full mode "adds ML-enabled image cleaning capabilities (erase stains, fingers, etc…)".
- **F2 (found, A).** A caption reads "Scanned document auto-rotated with finger removed and auto filter applied."
- **F3 (found, A).** Editing functions include "crop, apply filters, remove shadows, clean stains".
- **F4 (found, A).** Glare: **not mentioned** on the page. This is a silence on one page, not a finding that ML Kit does not remove glare.
- **F5 (found, A).** "The entire document scanner flow operates on-device." The ML models "are delivered using Google Play services".
- **F6 (found, A).** "Low binary size impact (all ML models and large resources are downloaded centrally in Google Play services)." No model size is given.
- **F7 (found, B).** Handover row #307, ML Kit as a second engine: "Not started". Note: "Cannot be tried without a phone with Google Play services."
- **F8 (found, A).** The page's content licence: "Except as otherwise noted, the content of this page is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 License." This is the documentation's licence; the ML Kit SDK's own terms were not read.

**Worked out.** ML Kit confirms, from a second source, that finger removal and stain cleaning are in production on-device scanners. It says nothing about glare, and it gives no sizes, speeds or method. So the claim "fingers and stains can be done on-device" is found; the method, quality and cost are **not known**.

## 2. Safety principle (from the spec, checked against the handover)

The spec's principle (never synthesise text; fill only blank paper) is a design choice. No public source was found that establishes it. The handover says nothing against it. I agree that a learned fill can invent content, and I found no source that makes a learned fill safe over text.

**Worked out (ring width).** The spec's blank-fill test uses a ring 2–6% of the page width. At a 2,000 px wide working page: 0.02 × 2,000 = 40 px and 0.06 × 2,000 = 120 px. The 6 px feather is 6 / 2,000 = 0.3% of the width. These are the spec's numbers at one working size; the size actually used by the app is not known to me.

## 3. Fingers

- **Not known:** detection precision and recall on 40 finger photos and 100 without (the spec's measure). The owner's photos are not available to me.
- **Not known:** whether the YCbCr skin range relative to the paper's white point (the spec) separates fingers from warm paper. No source read gives the range.
- **Not known:** whether a ring of 2–6% of the page width reliably shows ink under a finger.
- **Stated by the spec (not checked):** three of the owner's ten reference photos have fingers, and one commercial scanner kept them in natural skin tone. This is the owner's data, cited by the spec; I cannot check it.

**Worked out (what the spec's fill can and cannot do).** The fill is safe only where the ring is blank. A finger over text is flagged by the spec, not filled. So the ship candidate with the least risk is the **flag only** ("A finger covers part of the text: retake"), which needs the detector but no fill.

## 4. Glare

- **Not known:** how often a capture-time warning ("Glare on the page: tilt the phone or turn off the torch") removes glare. No source found.
- **Not known:** whether the two-shot merge (S11) works. No source read.
- **Not known:** whether a single-image fill inside a blown-out core, surrounded by blank paper, looks clean. No source found; the spec makes this a measured question.
- **Stated by the spec (not checked):** the glare test uses "two channels ≥ 250" and a halo. The halo rule and the 250 level are the spec's choices, unsourced.

**Worked out (clipping).** An 8-bit channel runs from 0 to 255. The levels 250 to 255 are six of 256 levels, 6 / 256 = 2.3% of the range. This arithmetic is correct; whether 250 separates glare from white paper is **not known**.

## 5. Stains

- **Found (A).** ML Kit's editing list includes "clean stains" (F3). It gives no method.
- **Not known:** whether per-channel background division inside a stain mask (the spec's method) removes a coffee ring or yellowed tape without touching ink. No source read. The spec measures it on owner samples.
- **Not known:** whether the chroma (0.05) and luminance (8%) rules separate stains from coloured paper.
- Inpainting or learned stain removal: I did not search for licences or sizes of any learned inpainting model (for example LaMa). The spec excludes generative fill over text, so such a model is only relevant for blank-only regions, where the classical fill already applies. I did not check it further.

## 6. Recommendation per defect

- **Fingers: do not fill for now; go only for the flag.** The fill needs a detector whose accuracy is **not known**, and the spec ranks it last. Ship the flag only if the detector clears the spec's 40/100 test on the owner's photos.
- **Glare: go only for the capture-time hint, and only if** it is measured to remove most glare on the owner's photos. Do not ship a single-image fill: the clipped core carries no information, and no source shows the fill looks clean.
- **Stains: go only if** the per-channel normalisation keeps ink contrast inside the stain within the spec's 15% and brings the paper inside within 6 levels of the paper outside (the spec's own tests). These are not known; the method needs no learned model, so it is the cheapest of the three to try.

## 7. Cheapest experiment that settles what is not known

1. **Synthetic tests first (desktop, no photos).** The spec's own synthetic tests: a finger blob on a blank margin (filled) and over text (not filled, flagged); a semi-transparent brown stain over text (ink contrast loss under 15%); a glare spot over blank paper (filled) and over text (flagged); no firing on clean pages (false positive rate under 2%). These do not settle real-world performance, but they check the code's logic before any photo is used.
2. **Owner's photos (the only real test).** The spec's 40 finger and 100 clean photos, plus the stained and glare samples. The numbers that decide: detector precision and recall, and for stains the cer and the paper-white change inside the stain. These come from the owner's data; I have none.
3. **Capture hint test.** On the owner's device, count how often a glare photo is retaken after the warning. The number that decides whether the hint is enough.
